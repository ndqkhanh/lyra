*Code: https://github.com/yjwtheonly/SciCustom*

## 1. Mechanism

SciCustom is an **offline-online framework** that automates construction of customized scientific benchmarks for LLM evaluation without expert annotation. The core insight: complex scientific applications can be approximated as **compositions of reusable knowledge units**.

### Offline Phase: Knowledge Unit Indexing

**Step 1 — Ontology-Grounded Knowledge Unit Selection (Algorithm 1):**
- Start with a comprehensive scientific ontology organized as DAGs spanning **227 subdisciplines** (from OBO, BioPortal, OLS).
- DFS traversal over each DAG. At each node v, an LLM classifies the term into one of three granularities: *coarse* (continue recursion), *moderate* (add to knowledge units T), or *fine* (prune branch).
- If |Desc(v)| < 10, backtrack (not enough sub-concepts to be a unit).
- Result: **641 scientific knowledge units** + 1 dedicated NON-SCIENTIFIC unit = **642 total**. Granularity comparable to "textbook chapter titles."

**Step 2 — Tagger Training (2-stage):**
- Stage 1 (synthetic): Randomly sample 1–5 knowledge units to form a target set Tq. For each unit, aggregate all descendant ontology nodes to extract keywords. Combine keywords into a prompt for an LLM (GPT-4o) to generate a synthetic query q. Label: ⟨q, Tq⟩. Result: **50,000 synthetic training queries**.
- Stage 2 (real-world): Sample scientific queries from existing instruction-tuning datasets. An LLM annotates each with corresponding knowledge units. Result: **30,000 real-world training queries**.
- Also include non-scientific queries for boundary modeling.
- Train a **fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B** tagger on 80K total instances, 2 epochs, lr=2e-5.
- Tagger maps query q → subset of knowledge units Tq ⊆ T. Macro F1: **75.2%**, Micro F1: **78.6%** on 1,000 unseen compositional queries.

**Step 3 — Once-for-all Corpus Population:**
- Aggregate scientific QA corpus from SciRIFF, SciInstruct, Mol-Instruct, MultiMedQA, SciEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, IfBench, SimpleQA → **2,000,367 total instances**.
- Run tagger on each instance to assign knowledge units. Filter out any instances overlapping with ground-truth benchmarks.

### Online Phase: Benchmark Generation

**Step 4 — Multi-Model Voting for Knowledge Unit Selection:**
- Given user requirement r (e.g., "Technical Chemistry benchmark").
- Three heterogeneous LLMs (GPT-5, Claude-opus-4.5, Gemini-3-pro-preview) independently rank candidate knowledge units by relevance to r.
- Aggregate via average rank position. Select **top-K1=10** units with lowest consensus rank → target set Tr.

**Step 5 — Binary Search-Based Relevance Filtering (Algorithm 2):**
- Construct candidate set Dr' = {⟨q,a⟩ ∈ D | |Tr ∩ Tq| ≥ 1}.
- Sort Dr' by: (1) decreasing |Tr,q|, then (2) increasing average rank R̄r(d) of matched units.
- Binary search over sorted LDr' to find the **cutoff** — the last position where a majority of the LLM ensemble judges the sample as relevant to r.
- Complexity: **O(log|Dr'|)** LLM calls instead of O(|Dr'|). Typical |Dr'| ~200K.
- Important design choice: binary search deliberately **bypasses the top-ranked instances** (which tend to be "canonical textbook knowledge" memorized by all LLMs, causing a performance ceiling). This captures a more challenging, discriminative distribution.

**Step 6 — Proxy Subset Selection (SubLIME-inspired):**
- For each d = ⟨q,a⟩ in Dr, compute:
  - **Hardness score H(d):** exp of negative average log-perplexity of answer tokens conditioned on question (open-weights LLM).
  - **Quality score Q(d):** Flesch Readability Score.
- Embed all pairs via RoBERTa into embedding space.
- Cluster embeddings, sample representatives to minimize **cumulative Wasserstein distance** between distributions of H and Q in the subset vs. full set.
- Select **K2=100** instances as proxy subset Pr.

**Step 7 — MCQ Generation:**
- LLM transforms raw QA pairs in Pr into standardized multiple-choice questions with plausible distractors.
- Distractor quality evaluation (500 MCQs, Claude-Opus-4.6 judge): Heuristic Transparency Score **8.7/10** (low leakage), Scientific Plausibility Score **7.9/10** (strong near-miss quality). Context-free baseline accuracy drops to **22%** (near random), confirming distractors are non-trivial.

## 2. Results

### Core Metric: Spearman/Kendall Rank Correlation with Expert Ground Truth
10 LLMs evaluated: Gemini-2.5-flash, GPT-4o, GPT-5-chat, Claude-opus-4.5, Qwen3-235b-a22b, Qwen3-max, Grok-4-fast-non-reasoning, Kimi-k2-preview, DeepSeek-V3.2, Mistral-large.

**Chemistry Domain (6 tasks) — Spearman ρ:**

| Task | SciCustom | Best Baseline | Baseline Name |
|------|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Analytical Chemistry | **0.86** | 0.61 | GPQA |
| Inorganic Chemistry | **0.67** | 0.52 | GPQA |
| Material Science | **0.42** | 0.21 | GPQA |
| Organic Chemistry | **0.89** | 0.72 | GPQA |
| Physical Chemistry | **0.74** | 0.63 | MedQA |
| Technical Chemistry | **0.86** | 0.72 | MedQA |

General benchmarks (IfBench, SimpleQA) had **negative** correlations with expert rankings. MMLU ranged from -0.61 to 0.52.

**Healthcare Domain (5 tasks) — Spearman ρ:**

| Task | SciCustom | Best Baseline |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| Virology | **0.55** | 0.44 (MedQA) |
| Human Aging | **0.49** | 0.62 (MedQA) — note: SciCustom is second |
| Medical Genetics | **0.42** | 0.35 (MedQA) |
| Anatomy | **0.62** | 0.48 (GPQA) |
| Nutrition | **0.78** | 0.56 (MMLU) |

SciCustom achieves **best or near-best** in 10/11 tasks. Top-1 model matches ground truth in **8/11 tasks**.

### Component Ablation (Figure 4):
- **w/o cutoff** (remove binary search): substantial degradation across all 11 subdisciplines.
- **w/o selection** (random sampling instead of proxy selection): even larger drop, confirming subset selection is critical.
- **Greedy Top-K vs Binary Search (Table 4):** Greedy falls to Spearman 0.16–0.34 on healthcare, vs SciCustom 0.42–0.78. Binary search captures more challenging distribution by avoiding canonical instances.

### Alternative Baselines (Appendix D):
- **GPT-5-RAG:** Spearman -0.02 to 0.41 (chemistry), 0.02 to 0.52 (healthcare)
- **Embedding-filtering** (k-NN + same cutoff/selection): Spearman 0.04 to 0.37 (chemistry), 0.13 to 0.51 (healthcare)
- Both consistently below SciCustom, confirming ontology-grounded knowledge units are the key differentiator.

### Pericyclic Reaction Case Study:
Successfully constructed benchmark for a domain with **zero prior benchmarks**. Top-ranked units (Cyclization, Aromatic hydrocarbon, Ring compound) highly correlated with expert assessment. Retrieved data and generated MCQs accurately capture domain terminology and Woodward-Hoffmann orbital symmetry principles.

### Human Evaluation:
- 50 chemistry MCQs, 3 Master's AI4Chemistry annotators.
- **Correctness: 0.92** (scientific accuracy). **Relevance: 0.70** (alignment with target requirement).

### Material Science Special Case:
Lowest correlation (0.42). Two *expert-curated* benchmarks themselves had Spearman **ρ=0.31** — indicating the domain is inherently heterogeneous and multi-faceted.

## 3. Trade-offs

| Win | Lose |
|-----|------|
| **No expert annotation needed** — fully automated pipeline | **Ontology-dependent** — currently only biomedical/chemical domains (OBO, BioPortal, OLS); no math/physics |
| **Scalable to any custom requirement** via reusable knowledge units | **Corpus-dependent** — benchmark quality bounded by source data coverage; low-resource knowledge units exist |
| **Data-grounded** (real scientific facts) vs hallucinated synthetic benchmarks | **2M+ corpus required** offline; cannot work from scratch |
| **Reveals fine-grained differences** standard benchmarks miss | **Material Science correlation only 0.42** — domain heterogeneity limits even expert benchmarks |
| **Efficient online phase** — O(log n) API calls via binary search | **Binary search assumes monotonic relevance** — theoretically imperfect (acknowledged by authors) |
| **Reusable mappings** — once-for-all tagger avoids re-processing corpus | **K2=100 proxy subset** limits benchmark size; some nuances may be lost |
| **Quantifiable distractor quality** (HTS 8.7, SPS 7.9) | **MCQ-only** format; no free-response, multi-step reasoning, or interactive evaluation |
| **Cross-model voting** reduces individual LLM bias in unit selection | **Requires 3 strong LLMs** (GPT-5, Claude, Gemini) for voting — API cost for novel requirements |

## 4. Design Rationale

**Why knowledge units instead of direct retrieval?** Direct semantic retrieval (Embedding baseline) fails because scientific requirements involve *intersectional* knowledge — a single requirement spans multiple subfields. Knowledge units provide explicit compositional semantics that embedding similarity cannot capture. The authors show Embedding scores -0.73 ρ for Physical Chemistry while SciCustom achieves 0.74.

**Why binary search instead of greedy top-K?** Counter-intuitively, the *most* relevant instances (highest keyword overlap) are "canonical textbook knowledge" exposed during pretraining, creating a ceiling effect where all models score near-perfect. Binary search finds a cutoff deeper in the sorted list, capturing more challenging, discriminative instances.

**Why a small trained tagger instead of LLM-based annotation?** The corpus contains 2M+ instances. Even with the cheapest API models, annotating each instance would be prohibitively expensive and slow. A fine-tuned LLaMA-3-8B provides scalable inference at a fraction of the cost while maintaining reasonable accuracy (F1 78.6%).

**Why ontology-grounded granularity control?** Too coarse: cannot distinguish fine-grained model capabilities. Too fine: knowledge units become non-reusable across requirements. The "textbook chapter title" granularity (642 units) was empirically chosen to balance discrimination and reuse.

**Why grounded data instead of synthetic generation?** Scientific evaluation requires verifiable correctness. Synthetic questions (GPT-5 baseline) risk hallucination and cannot guarantee factual validity. Grounding in real scientific literature provides verifiability.

## 5. Limitations & Failure Modes

1. **Ontology scope constraint:** Only covers biomedical and chemical domains. Mathematics, theoretical physics, computer science, and engineering disciplines not yet supported. The framework's effectiveness depends entirely on the quality and breadth of the underlying ontology.

2. **Data sparsity for niche knowledge units:** Some of the 642 knowledge units may have few or no data instances after tagger mapping. The authors acknowledge this and propose frequency monitoring as a future mitigation, but no solution is implemented.

3. **Non-monotonic relevance assumption:** Binary search assumes monotonic decreasing relevance in the sorted candidate list, which is theoretically violated. The authors validate this empirically works better than greedy, but no theoretical guarantee exists.

4. **Material Science remains challenging:** Even expert benchmarks disagree (ρ=0.31 inter-expert correlation). SciCustom's 0.42 correlation, while best among methods, is still low in absolute terms. Domain heterogeneity is a fundamental unsolved problem.

5. **MCQ-only evaluation format:** The framework only produces multiple-choice questions, which cannot assess free-response reasoning, multi-step problem solving, code generation, or interactive scientific workflows.

6. **English-only:** The ontology and corpus are exclusively English. Multilingual scientific evaluation is not addressed.

7. **Static benchmark generation:** Once generated, benchmarks are static. No mechanism for adaptive difficulty or iterative refinement based on model performance.

8. **Proxy subset may exclude rare but important instances:** K2=100 constraint means the benchmark captures the *distribution* of difficulty/quality from the full candidate set, but novel edge cases or rare scientific facts may be omitted.

9. **Tagger F1 ceiling at 78.6%:** Misclassification of query-to-knowledge-unit mapping propagates errors through the entire pipeline.

## 6. Transfer to Lyra

**The one transferable idea:** SciCustom's **ontology-grounded knowledge unit decomposition with a lightweight tagger** for mapping user instructions into fine-grained knowledge subspaces can be repurposed for **Lyra's routing and tool-selection subsystem**. Instead of relying on coarse intent classification (which fails for novel domains), Lyra could maintain a DAG of task-capability knowledge units and train a small tagger to map incoming queries into compositions of these units, enabling: (1) dynamic routing to the appropriate tools/models based on the knowledge unit composition, (2) retrieval of relevant examples, tools, and prior interaction patterns from a pre-indexed interaction corpus, and (3) adaptive self-evaluation benchmark construction for continuous capability assessment.

**Workstream route:** §4.2 (Context & Routing — the knowledge unit mapping mechanism directly feeds the router's ability to decompose scientific/technical tasks into fine-grained capability requirements).

**Assessment:**
- **Impact:** 3 (moderate — ontology construction is a significant upfront cost, but the routing improvement in specialized domains would be substantial)
- **Effort:** 3 (requires ontology curation, tagger training, corpus indexing — manageable with existing infrastructure)
- **Tier:** investigate (promising architecture pattern, but needs domain-specific ontology work before implementation; worth prototyping with a limited subdomain first)
