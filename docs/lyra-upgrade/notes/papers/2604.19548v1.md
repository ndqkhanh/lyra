## 1. Mechanism (step-by-step)

### Problem: Actor-Observer Asymmetry (AOA)

In multi-agent systems with role-playing (executor vs. reviewer), agents exhibit a human-like cognitive bias:
- **Actor** (self-reflecting executor): Attributes failures to **external** factors ("the context was missing", "the instructions were vague")
- **Observer** (auditing reviewer): Attributes the same failures to **internal** faults ("the agent made a logic error", "the agent is incompetent")

This is quantified via the **Ambiguous Failure Benchmark (AFB)** — 200 synthetic interaction traces across 10 domains where failure attribution is genuinely ambiguous between internal and external causes. The key metric is **Flip** (percentage of cases where attribution shifts solely due to role-swap) and **V-AOA** (Vanilla AOA: actor externalizes, observer internalizes).

### Stage 1: Attribution Data Generation

Constructs failure attribution datasets from FinQA (hybrid reasoning over financial data) and Spider (text-to-SQL) using a retrieval-augmented pipeline with Qwen-2.5-7B:

1. **Context Retrieval**: For question Q and corpus C, retrieve top-k evidence items E
2. **Program Synthesis**: Generate executable logic to produce answer a-hat
3. **Fact-Check Labeling**: Compare against gold evidence E_gold and correct answer a*:
   - **FalseExt**: E_gold not subset of E (evidence missing — external fault)
   - **FalseInt**: E_gold subset of E but a-hat ≠ a* (reasoning error — internal fault)
   - **True**: E_gold subset of E and a-hat = a* (correct)

### Stage 2: Dialectical Synthesis (TAS)

Uses GPT-5.1 as teacher to generate **Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis** trajectories:

```
<thinking>
  <thesis>[Role-Dependent Bias: initial blaming reaction]</thesis>
  <antithesis>[Evidence Verification: challenges the thesis against retrieved facts]</antithesis>
  <synthesis>[Objective Convergence: resolves conflict by grounding in evidence]</synthesis>
</thinking>
[Attribution] FalseExt | FalseInt | True
[Action] Search(new_query) | Revise(code) | Confirm()
```

Two opposing role trajectories are generated per question — Defensive Actor and Critical Reviewer — both required to converge to the same synthesized attribution grounded in evidence.

### Stage 3: Dialectical Alignment

Backbone: **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507** (4B parameters)

**Phase A — Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**:
- 3 epochs, LR = 5e-6
- Standard cross-entropy on synthesized dialectical corpus
- Teaches TAS format and action vocabulary

**Phase B — Reinforcement Alignment (GRPO)**:
- Group Relative Policy Optimization with group size G=8 trajectories
- Batch size 1, gradient accumulation 16 steps
- 750 optimization steps (~1.9 epochs on FinQA-TAS, ~1.7 on Spider-TAS)

**Composite Reward Function:**
```
R(·) = α·R1(·) + β·R2(·) + γ·R3(·)
```
- R1: Format reward (correct TAS structure) — α = 1
- R2: Attribution-matching reward (label matches ground truth) — β = 2
- R3: Answer-correctness reward — γ = 4

### Key Design Decisions:
- GRPO is **critical**: SFT alone cannot internalize dialectical reasoning as a behavioral habit
- All three reward components are **indispensable** — removing any one causes significant degradation
- TAS format is **model-agnostic**: improves all tested models (4B–32B) when applied as zero-shot prompting

## 3. Trade-offs

| Wins | Losses |
|------|--------|
| 4B model beats 30B+ baselines on attribution accuracy and bias | Does not match GPT-5.1/DeepSeek-V3.2 on raw downstream F1 (76.9 vs 72.1) |
| V-AOA reduced by 3x–5x vs. prompting baselines | Requires training (not a zero-shot solution); GRPO needs 9 hours on dual H200 GPUs |
| Cross-domain generalization: ReTAS trained on FinQA transfers to AFB with strong bias reduction | TAS trajectory generation depends on a closed-source teacher (GPT-5.1); quality bounded by teacher |
| Structured reasoning improves downstream task performance, not just bias metrics | Limited to retrieval-augmented reasoning domains; open-ended creative tasks untested |
| Parameter-efficient: deployable on consumer hardware | 4B backbone has absolute ceiling below frontier models |
| Dialectical format is model-agnostic (improves all tested models as prompting template) | Dual View alone can be actively harmful — naive role-diversification worsens outcomes |

## 4. Design Rationale

**Why Fichtean dialectics (Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis)?** Naive interventions fail because: (a) "Be objective" prompts trigger defensive justifications due to role inertia; (b) forcing opposing perspectives invites over-correction and groundless self-blame. Both treat symptoms rather than the underlying role-induced cognitive prior. Fichte's three-stage dialectic explicitly decouples the agent's role-based defense mechanism from objective ground truth — each stage serves a distinct purpose (surface bias, challenge it, synthesize evidence-grounded consensus).

**Why GRPO instead of just SFT?** Prompting alone cannot enforce structured reasoning as a behavioral habit. SFT teaches the format ("knowing how") but GRPO internalizes the dialectical policy ("doing it automatically"). The ablation (SFT-only drops Acc 71.2→67.7, doubles attribution errors) confirms this.

**Why Qwen3-4B?** Deliberately small backbone to demonstrate parameter efficiency — full fine-tuning is affordable, highly deployable, and proves the method works via alignment rather than scale. The method is model-agnostic by design.

**Why three reward components?** R2 (attribution matching) is the key anti-bias signal; without it, V-AOA triples. R3 (answer correctness) ensures the dialectical process improves actual task outcomes, not just attribution metrics. R1 (format) provides the structural scaffold.

## 5. Limitations & Failure Modes

1. **Narrow diagnostic scope:** Training and evaluation limited to FinQA-TAS and Spider-TAS (retrieval-augmented reasoning). Open-ended planning, creative generation, and long-horizon tasks — where objective fault attribution is inherently subjective — remain untested.
2. **Synthetic data dependency:** Both AFB and training trajectories are synthesized (GPT-5.1). Real-world human-agent failure logs may contain distributional properties not captured by synthetic generators. No in-domain real data validation.
3. **Sales Arena is only an initial probe:** Multi-round negotiation tested in a constrained 4-item, fixed-budget setting. Broader negotiation dynamics (asymmetric information, reputation effects, coalition formation) unexplored.
4. **Teacher model bias:** GPT-5.1 shows 94% internal attribution bias in AFB — if the teacher that generates TAS trajectories inherits this bias, the training data may encode systemic skew.
5. **GRPO requires non-trivial compute:** 9 hours on dual H200 GPUs for a 4B model; scaling to 70B+ would be expensive.
6. **No exploration of alternative dialectical frameworks:** Only Fichtean dialectics tested; Hegelian or other synthesis paradigms may perform differently.
7. **Binary attribution (Int/Ext) oversimplifies real failures:** Real-world fault localization often involves partial contributions from multiple factors — the forced-choice design loses this granularity.

## 6. Transfer to Lyra

### The One Idea
**Dialectical Self-Correction via Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis with GRPO alignment.** When Lyra agents engage in self-reflection or peer review, role-induced attribution bias systematically skews fault diagnosis. Replace naive "reflect and fix" loops with a structured three-stage reasoning process: (1) surface the role-congruent bias (Thesis), (2) challenge it against evidence (Antithesis), (3) synthesize an evidence-grounded conclusion (Synthesis). Train via GRPO with a composite reward that penalizes perspective-inconsistent attributions.

### Workstream Route
**§4.2 — Reliability / Self-Correction / Verification.** This directly addresses the verification pipeline where Lyra's executor and reviewer agents need to converge on objective fault attribution. Also feeds **§4.7 — Safety** (preventing biased self-assessment from masking real failures).

### Assessment

| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Impact | **4/5** | Biased self-correction is a known failure mode in agent loops; dialectical alignment provides a principled fix with strong empirical evidence |
| Effort | **4/5** | Requires: (a) constructing Lyra-specific attribution dataset, (b) generating TAS trajectories with teacher model, (c) GRPO training. Not a simple prompt change |
| Tier | **investigate** | Strong paper but synthetic-only evaluation and narrow domain scope mean Lyra should prototype on a single verification subtask before committing. The core idea (structured dialectical reflection) is sound and immediately applicable as a prompting pattern even without training |
