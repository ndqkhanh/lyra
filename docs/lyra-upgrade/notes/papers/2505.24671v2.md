# Multiple LLM Agents Debate for Equitable Cultural Alignment
*Dayeon Ki, Rachel Rudinger, Tianyi Zhou, Marine Carpuat — University of Maryland*
*arXiv:2505.24671v2 [cs.CL], 1 Sep 2025 — https://github.com/dayeonki/cultural_debate*

## 1. Mechanism (step-by-step)

The paper proposes a **Multi-Agent Debate framework** with two LLM-based agents debating cultural scenarios and collaboratively reaching a final decision. Four strategies are tested on the NORMAD-ETI benchmark (2.6K stories, 75 countries, ternary labels: Yes/No/Neither).

### Strategy A — Single-LLM Baselines

**1. Single Model:** An LLM receives the country, rule-of-thumb, and story as input and directly predicts a ternary label. Cultural contextualization adds the rule-of-thumb to the prompt, which alone improves accuracy by 28.7–48.4% across models.

**2. Self-Reflection:** An LLM \(M\) (a) generates initial decision \(\hat{y}_0^M\) by evaluating the cultural scenario, (b) generates a verbal rationale \(f^M\) for its decision, and (c) incorporates the rationale as additional context to make a final decision \(\hat{y}_f^M\). This is an iterative single-agent improvement step.

### Strategy B — Multi-LLM Debate-Only

Two LLM agents \(M_1\) and \(M_2\):

1. **Initial decisions:** Both independently generate \(\hat{y}_0^{M_1}, \hat{y}_0^{M_2}\) given the same inputs (country, rule-of-thumb, story).
2. **Feedback exchange:** Agent \(M_1\) generates feedback \(f^{M_1}\) on \(M_2\)'s initial decision, and vice versa. Feedback is exchanged symmetrically.
3. **Final decisions:** Each agent incorporates the other's feedback and initial decision to produce final decisions:

\[
\hat{y}_f^{M_i} = M_i(\hat{y}_0^{M_i}, \hat{y}_0^{j}, f^{M_i}, f^{M_j}) \quad \text{for } i=1, j=2 \text{ (and vice versa)}
\]

4. **Disagreement resolution:** If \(\hat{y}_f^{M_1} = \hat{y}_f^{M_2}\), that answer is used. Otherwise, a judge LLM \(M_j\) (Gemma-2-27B) adjudicates:

\[
M_j(\hat{y}_0^{M_1}, \hat{y}_0^{M_2}, f^{M_1}, f^{M_2}, \hat{y}_f^{M_1}, \hat{y}_f^{M_2}) \rightarrow \hat{y}_f^{j}
\]

Agents are exchangeable — symmetrical access to all information. Default: one round of debate (single feedback exchange).

### Strategy C — Self-Reflect+Debate

Each agent dynamically chooses during its turn between:
- **(A) Self-reflection:** Generate a reflection \(r^{M_i}\) on its own previous output, or
- **(B) Debate:** Generate feedback \(f^{M_i}\) on the discussant's output.

If \(M_1\) self-reflects and \(M_2\) debates:

\[
\hat{y}_f^{M_i} = M_i(\hat{y}_0^{M_i}, \hat{y}_0^{j}, r^{M_1}, f^{M_2})
\]

Choice is made via multiple-choice (A/B) format, repeated twice with alternating option order to mitigate position bias. Judge LLM resolves remaining disagreements.

### Cultural Group Parity Metric

Parity is operationalized as the ratio of a group's mean accuracy to that of the best-performing baseline group \(b\) (English-speaking):

\[
\text{Parity}(g) = \frac{\text{Acc}_g}{\text{Acc}_b}
\]

A method achieves parity for group \(g\) when \(\text{Parity}(g) \approx 1\).

### Models Deployed

7 open-weight LLMs (7-9B) across language-coverage tiers: English-centric (LLaMA-3, Gemma-2), Bilingual EN-KO (EXAONE-3), Bilingual EN-ZH (Yi-1.5, InternLM-2.5), Multilingual 23 langs (AYA-23), Multilingual 12 East Asian (SeaLLM-3). Judge: Gemma-2-27B. All instruction-tuned. Temperature = 0.0.

Oracle routing (using ground truth labels to pick the correct prediction between two models) improves accuracy by avg **+22.5%** (up to **+41.7%** for EXAONE-3+AYA-23), demonstrating strong complementary knowledge across diverse LLMs.

### Decision Dynamics (Figure 2)
- Self-Reflection: Initial correct 62.0% → Final correct 65.3% (small fix, some regressions).
- Debate-Only: Mixed initial 56.5% → Both correct final 63.7% → Judge correct 76.3%.
- Self-Reflect+Debate: Mixed initial 56.0% → Both correct final 56.7% → Judge correct 75.6%.

The debate process converts most "mixed" (disagreed) initial states to correct outcomes. The judge LLM is critical: it raises accuracy from ~60% (agent agreement) to ~76% (after adjudication).

## 3. Trade-offs (wins vs loses)

| Win | Lose |
|---|---|
| +7.05% avg accuracy over single-LLM (Debate-Only) | 6× computational cost for Debate-Only vs Single Model |
| 7-9B model pairs match 27B model (79.7% ≈ 79.2%) | 8× cost for Self-Reflect+Debate variant |
| Best cultural parity (0.972-0.994) of all methods | Self-Reflection sometimes beats individual debate agent accuracy (9/21 settings) |
| Debate corrects 56.5% mixed → 76.3% correct via judge | Self-Reflect+Debate sometimes degrades individual accuracy (14/21 vs 19/21 settings) |
| Works with open-weight models (no API dependency) | Judge LLM (27B) required for disagreement resolution |
| Improves most on "Neither" labels (where LLMs are weakest) | Confucian group remains worst (19.1% correct even after debate) |
| Single debate round sufficient — no diminishing returns | Dead loops with multi-round debate (LLMs fail to progress discussion) |
| Debater agents are exchangeable — no role engineering needed | Oracle gap: 3.6% between debate + judge and perfect oracle resolution |

## 4. Design Rationale (why this approach)

1. **Diverse training data creates complementary knowledge:** Open-weight LLMs have varying language coverage, alignment processes, and data distributions. No single model supports all cultures equally well — the best-performing model varies across countries (Appendix C.2).

2. **Debate exploits complementarity without training:** Rather than fine-tuning on culturally diverse data (expensive, data-scarce), debate combines existing inference-time knowledge from multiple models. This is a "society of minds" approach adapted specifically for cultural alignment.

3. **Self-reflection has known failure modes:** Single-model self-reflection suffers from confirmation bias and hallucination. Debate introduces an external critic, breaking the self-consistency loop.

4. **Judge LLM as a necessity, not luxury:** Since agents disagree in ~56% of initial decisions, a strong adjudicator is essential. The judge summarizes debate history rather than making independent judgments — this constrains it to the evidence presented.

5. **One round is optimal:** More rounds introduce dead loops where LLMs fail to progress discussion. The marginal benefit of additional rounds is zero or negative.

## 5. Limitations & Failure Modes

1. **Single dataset / single task type:** NORMAD-ETI has only 30-40 stories per country and uses ternary classification (Yes/No/Neither). Results may not generalize to other cultural alignment tasks, more nuanced decision types, or open-ended generation.

2. **Confucian group remains poorly served:** Even after debate, Confucian cultural group accuracy is only ~19% correct final decisions (vs 68% for English-speaking). Debate reduces but does not eliminate cultural bias.

3. **Self-Reflect+Debate can be harmful:** In 7/21 settings, Self-Reflect+Debate individual accuracy drops below single-model baseline. InternLM-2.5 particularly degrades (e.g., drops from 67.8% to 28.3% in one pairing). The mechanism for when self-reflection + debate helps vs hurts is not characterized.

4. **Judge LLM dependency:** Results rely on a single strong judge (Gemma-2-27B). The framework's performance with weaker judges or conflicting judge-debater model families is untested. Oracle gap remains 3.6% — the judge does not perfectly resolve all disagreements.

5. **Position bias in Self-Reflect+Debate choice:** LLMs' choice between self-reflection and debate varies with option ordering (mitigated by two-run averaging, but not eliminated).

6. **Computational overhead:** 6-8× the cost of single-model inference, plus a larger judge model. Cost scales with debater model size.

7. **Ternary classification only:** Does not capture degrees of cultural conformity, contextual nuance, or open-ended cultural reasoning.

8. **Only 7-9B models tested:** Results may not hold for much smaller or much larger debater models. The complementarity dynamic discovered may be specific to this scale.

## 6. Transfer to Lyra

**The One Transferable Idea:** Use multi-model debate as an inference-time disagreement resolver to improve accuracy on tasks where no single small model achieves parity with a large model — specifically, deploy two 7-9B open-weight models in a single-round debate with a larger judge model to close the accuracy gap while maintaining lower total cost than running a single very large model.

**Why this matters for Lyra:** Lyra's evaluation harness can integrate debate-based disagreement resolution when model confidence is low or when different models give conflicting answers. This is particularly valuable for culturally/linguistically diverse user queries where a single model may have systematic blind spots. The mechanism is inference-only (no fine-tuning), making it immediately deployable.

**Workstream Route:** §4.2 (Evaluation & Quality Gates) — debate-as-judge pattern for model output verification; also feeds §4.1 (Core Agent Architecture) if Lyra agents are paired for collaborative reasoning on ambiguous inputs.

**Impact:** 4 — Debate reliably improves accuracy (~+7%) AND cultural equity, with a proven open-weight implementation.
**Effort:** 2 — Inference-only method; requires no training data, no model modification. Only prompt engineering + orchestration logic.
**Tier:** **investigate** — The computational cost (6× inference) and judge dependency need careful ROI analysis for Lyra's specific latency/cost budget, but the approach is low-risk and immediately testable. The complementarity finding (different models excel for different cultures) is independently valuable: Lyra can route culturally-specific queries to the best-performing model without full debate.
