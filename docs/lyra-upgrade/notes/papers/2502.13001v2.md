## 1. Mechanism (step-by-step)

MIMIC (Multi-agent IMItation of Conversations) is a 7-stage pipeline inspired by movie production, divided into three phases. The core idea: summarize a knowledge source, then orchestrate an LLM debate among psychologically grounded persona-agents to produce a realistic meeting transcript.

### Phase 1: Pre-Production (Stages 1-3)

**Stage 1 -- Content Brainstorming:**
- Input: a knowledge source text (Wikipedia article, research paper, story)
- An LLM extracts hierarchical topics and subtopics following Paoli (2023).
- The LLM composes an abstractive "target summary" in QMSum style, guided by five human-written QMSum summaries for consistent brevity, style, and structure.
- This summary defines the discussion's topic flow and acts as a basic outline.

**Stage 2 -- Casting:**
- An LLM iteratively creates participant profiles. Each profile contains:
  - A functional role (e.g., project manager, technical expert)
  - Background (experience, qualifications)
  - Domain expertise
  - A distinct perspective (e.g., favoring practical solutions)
- Profiles are generated one at a time, ensuring complementary viewpoints without redundancy.
- Each participant is assigned psychologically grounded behavioral roles drawn from Benne & Sheats (1948). These fall into two categories:
  - **Group behaviors:** Initiator-Contributor, Information Giver, Information Seeker, Opinion Giver, Opinion Seeker, Coordinator, Evaluator-Critic, Implementer, Recorder, Encourager, Harmonizer, Compromiser, Gatekeeper, Standard Setter, Group Observer, Follower
  - **Individual behaviors:** Aggressor, Blocker, Recognition Seeker, Dominator, Help Seeker, Special Interest Pleader
- An LLM checks for contradictory traits (e.g., proactive yet blocking).
- Each participant also gets a detailed speaking style profile (tone, language complexity, communication style, sentence structure, formality, personalized vocabulary including filler words, catchphrases, speech patterns, emotional expressions).

**Stage 3 -- Scripting:**
- An LLM expands the target summary into a flexible outline of thematic scenes.
- Each scene: title, TL;DR agenda description, bullet points of key content, relevant source material aspects, invitations for personal experience sharing.
- Extra scenes (e.g., pre-meeting brainstorming) may be added for realism.
- Output: a scene-by-scene roadmap allowing spontaneous discussion within a broader structure.

### Phase 2: Production (Stages 4-5)

**Stage 4 -- Filming (Multi-Agent Debate):**
- Each participant has **private memory** containing:
  - Their own profile (role, expertise, perspective, behaviors)
  - Relevant source snippets
  - Summary of prior scenes
  - Last 3 turns of the previous scene
  - All turns so far in the current scene
- The scene begins with the participant most relevant to the topic, determined by LLM-based matching (Li et al., 2025).
- Turn-by-turn dialogue:
  - Each turn: participant generates dialogue in 1-3 sentences with natural speech patterns.
  - After each turn: the current speaker **nominates the next speaker** based on roles, current focus, and prior contributions.
  - The speaker is reminded about other participants to prevent leaving anyone out.
  - Participants can call a **vote to end a scene**. If >50% agree, the scene ends.
  - A system reminder forces finalization if no vote after 50 turns.
  - MIMIC proceeds to the next scene or terminates if all scenes complete.

**Stage 5 -- Quality Assuring (Director Review):**
- A "director" LLM (inspired by Self-Refine, Madaan et al. 2023) reviews each scene along three dimensions:
  1. **Topical alignment** -- adherence to target summary (Lin and Chen, 2023)
  2. **Conversational naturalness** -- turn-taking quality, dialogue flow (Liu et al., 2023)
  3. **Coherence/factual accuracy** -- logical progression, consistency with knowledge source (Xie et al., 2024)
- The director breaks both summary and transcript into atomic facts, checks coverage, and provides feedback.
- If rejected, the scene is re-shot with director feedback integrated.

### Phase 3: Post-Production (Stages 6-7)

**Stage 6 -- Special Effects:**
- With 25% probability per scene, a special effect is injected:
  - Polite interruptions, overlapping speech, side comments
  - Technical difficulties ("You're on mute"), phone ringing
  - Time checks, misunderstandings, light-hearted jokes
  - External disruptions (notifications)
- Effects are tailored to meeting type and target language.

**Stage 7 -- Editing (Two-step refinement):**
- **Step 1 (Editor Refinement):** Removes repetitive phrases and overly formal speech. Adds natural speech markers (hesitations, incomplete sentences, colloquialisms). Ensures alignment with participant expertise/roles. Introduces natural meeting characteristics (interruptions, informal markers).
- **Step 2 (AI Content Detection + Humanizing):** A detector LLM identifies remaining synthetic cues (uniformly polite turns, unrealistic consensus, repetitive patterns). A revision LLM addresses each issue: introduces minor disagreements, paraphrases, or adjusts tone.

### Algorithm: The Conversation Loop

```
for each scene in meeting_outline:
    select starting_participant via LLM matching
    turn_count = 0
    while scene not ended:
        current_speaker generates turn (1-3 sentences)
        current_speaker optionally calls vote to end
        if vote called and majority agrees: break
        if turn_count > 50: force end with system reminder
        current_speaker nominates next_speaker
        turn_count += 1
    director evaluates scene (accept/reject + feedback)
    if rejected: re-shoot scene with feedback
    if random() < 0.25: inject special_effect
    scene = editor_refine(scene)
    scene = detect_and_humanize(scene)
```

### FAME Dataset Construction

- 300 Wikipedia articles from 28 broad domains (Global Issues, Tech Innovations, Cultural Diversity, Philosophy, Environment & Ecology)
- Selection criteria: >= 5 subsections, no negative quality flags, no reference deficiencies
- 500 English meetings + 300 German meetings (parallel input from same article pool)
- 14 meeting types: Brainstorming, Decision-Making, Problem-Solving, Training/Workshop, Strategic Planning, Committee/Board, Innovation Forum, Agile/Scrum, Remote/Virtual, Project Kick-Off, Stakeholder, Casual Catch-Up, Cross-Functional, Retrospective
- Backbone: GPT-4o (primary), DeepSeek, Gemini, Llama (tested alternatives)

| Win | Loss |
|-----|------|
| Near-real spontaneity: 4.5/5 naturalness matching real corpora | 35x more expensive than omniscient baseline ($7.63 vs $0.22/meeting) |

| Fine-tuning transfer: up to 1.5 MESA point improvement on real QMSum | Quality partially depends on backbone LLM; smaller models need more refinement passes |
| Modular 7-stage pipeline works with different backbones (GPT, DeepSeek, Llama) | Mid-scale models produce ~50 fewer turns, limiting conversational depth |
| Private memory per agent prevents omniscience artifacts (repetition, recitation) | Voting mechanism can cut scenes short before full topic exploration |
| Multi-dimensional evaluation (authenticity + behavioral + challenge) | Evaluation biases: self-assessment vs observation gap (Dunning-Kruger confound) |

## 4. Design Rationale

**Why multi-agent over omniscient single-model:** Omniscient single-LLM approaches produce shallow, repetitive dialogues, fail to simulate group behaviors (topic control, standpoint maintenance, support offering), and sometimes regurgitate source text verbatim. Multi-agent debate with private memory forces agents to rely on one another, creating realistic information asymmetry and turn-taking dynamics.

**Why psychology-grounded casting:** Serapio-Garcia et al. (2023) showed LLMs can exhibit personality traits. Benne & Sheats (1948) provides a validated taxonomy of 22 group/individual behaviors. Combining these ensures participants exhibit consistent, realistic interpersonal dynamics (conflict, power, status, trust, support) rather than arbitrary or uniformly cooperative behavior.

**Why the movie production metaphor:** Decomposing complex multi-party dialogue generation into pre-production (planning characters/script), production (executing scenes with feedback), and post-production (polishing with special effects) maps naturally to the problem structure. It separates concerns: content planning, persona design, execution, and quality control.

**Why Self-Refine for quality control:** Madaan et al. (2023) demonstrated that LLMs can iteratively improve their own outputs through self-feedback. The director model evaluates atomic fact coverage, naturalness, and coherence -- providing actionable feedback before costly human review.

**Why post-production editing:** Even well-generated transcripts carry synthetic cues (overly polite turns, formulaic transitions, uniform consensus). A detector-revision loop targets these specifically without requiring regeneration, which is efficient and preserves conversational content.

## 5. Limitations & Failure Modes

1. **Backbone LLM dependency:** Quality partially depends on the underlying LLM's capabilities and biases. Models with smaller context windows or different linguistic styles produce less coherent dialogues or more frequent artifacts. Mid-scale models (DeepSeek, Llama) generate ~50 fewer turns.

2. **Coverage gaps:** FAME covers seven broad Wikipedia domains and features a German subset, but does not encompass all real-world meeting types or cultural nuances (e.g., corporate etiquette in Japan, cross-cultural communication patterns, hierarchical organizational dynamics).

3. **Specialized domain exclusion:** Highly specialized domains (e.g., medical conferences with technical jargon, legal proceedings) remain outside scope. Research papers (arXiv, PubMed) tested but not in the main dataset.

4. **Synthetic artifacts (7% of meetings):** Overuse of transitional phrases ("This sounds great, but..."), overly cordial tones, and uniformity in politeness reveal synthetic origin in 57 out of 800 meetings.

5. **Voting mechanism limitations:** The majority-vote-to-end mechanism can truncate scenes prematurely if participants agree too quickly, potentially missing deeper discussion of subtopics.

6. **Cost barrier to scale:** At $7.63/meeting, generating large-scale datasets (e.g., 10K+ meetings) becomes expensive. The omniscient baseline at $0.22 is far cheaper but produces lower quality.

7. **Language limitation:** Only English and German. Languages with different conversational norms (e.g., Japanese keigo, Arabic diglossia) not tested.

8. **Evaluation confounds:** Self-assessment vs. observation gaps (Dunning-Kruger effect) may affect crowd-sourced baseline comparisons. Krippendorff's alpha (0.836) is acceptable but not perfect.

9. **"Blocker" role challenge:** GPT partially avoids the Blocker role due to alignment toward supportive language, limiting the range of negative behaviors expressible.

## 6. Transfer to Lyra

### The ONE Transferable Idea

**Psychology-grounded persona engineering for multi-agent conversations with private memory partitions.** MIMIC demonstrates that assigning validated psychological behavioral roles (Benne & Sheats 1948: Initiator, Evaluator-Critic, Blocker, etc.) combined with persona-specific speaking styles (filler words, catchphrases, emotional expressions) and private knowledge memory produces measurably authentic multi-agent interactions. Lyra can adopt this framework to give its agent personas distinct, psychologically coherent behavioral profiles that naturally generate realistic conversation dynamics -- conflict, trust, support, power negotiation -- rather than homogeneous cooperative dialogue.
